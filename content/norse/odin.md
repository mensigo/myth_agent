Odin is the chief god of the Norse pantheon, the Allfather, associated with wisdom, war, poetry, and death. He is a relentless seeker of knowledge: he sacrificed one of his eyes at Mímir's well for a draught of wisdom, and hung himself for nine nights upon the world-tree Yggdrasil, pierced by his own spear, to win the secret of the runes. Two ravens, Huginn ("thought") and Muninn ("memory"), fly over the world each day and return to whisper what they have seen.

Odin gathers slain warriors to his hall Valhalla, where they feast and fight in preparation for Ragnarök, the doom of the gods. He rides the eight-legged horse Sleipnir and wields the spear Gungnir, which never misses. Though a god of victory, he is also a restless wanderer and a master of seiðr (magic), often traveling the world in disguise to test mortals and gather knowledge.
