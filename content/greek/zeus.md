Zeus is the king of the Olympian gods and the ruler of the sky. Youngest of the six children of the Titans Cronus and Rhea, he escaped being swallowed by his father when Rhea hid him and gave Cronus a stone in his place. Grown to strength, Zeus freed his swallowed siblings and led them to victory over the Titans, after which the three brothers divided the cosmos: Zeus took the sky, Poseidon the sea, and Hades the underworld, with the earth held in common.

As sovereign, Zeus upholds cosmic and social order — oaths, the rights of guests and suppliants, and the boundaries of justice fall under his care, which is why he is invoked as Zeus Xenios, guardian of hospitality. He wields the thunderbolt forged by the Cyclopes, and his sacred bird is the eagle. Married to his sister Hera, he is also the father of many gods and heroes through numerous unions, including Athena, Apollo, Artemis, Ares, Hermes, Dionysus, and the mortal hero Heracles. His many love affairs and Hera's jealous response drive a great number of Greek myths.
