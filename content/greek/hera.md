Hera is the queen of the Olympian gods and the goddess of marriage, women, and the family. A daughter of Cronus and Rhea, she is both sister and wife to Zeus, and her marriage to him is treated as the divine model of wedlock. She presides over weddings and married women, and childbirth falls partly within her sphere through her daughter Eileithyia.

Hera is famous for her jealousy and her relentless persecution of Zeus's lovers and their offspring — Heracles, whose very name ("glory of Hera") is bound up with her hostility, spends much of his life laboring under her enmity. Her own children include Ares, god of war; Hephaestus, the smith-god; Hebe, cupbearer of the gods; and Eileithyia, goddess of childbirth. Her sacred animals are the peacock and the cow, and she is often called "ox-eyed" for her large, striking eyes.
