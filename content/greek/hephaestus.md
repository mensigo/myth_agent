Hephaestus is the god of fire, the forge, and craftsmanship — the divine smith and artisan of Olympus. By the common tradition he is the son of Hera, who in some versions bore him alone, and he is unique among the gods for being lame: cast out of Olympus (either by Hera, ashamed of his deformity, or by Zeus in a quarrel), he fell to earth and was left crippled. Despite this, he is a maker of unmatched skill, crafting the gods' palaces, Zeus's thunderbolts and scepter, Achilles' armor, and even automata of gold to assist him.

His workshops were imagined beneath volcanoes, where his fires burned. He is married to Aphrodite, and his best-known myth is his revenge on her and her lover Ares: warned of their affair, he forged a net of invisible chains, caught them together, and summoned the gods to witness their shame. In another tale he fashioned Pandora, the first woman, at Zeus's command. His tools — the hammer, anvil, and tongs — mark him as the patron of smiths and craftsmen.
