The Titanomachy is the great ten-year war by which the Olympian gods overthrew the Titans and won rule of the cosmos. It grows out of a cycle of generational conflict: the primordial sky-god Uranus was overthrown and castrated by his son Cronus, who then, warned that he too would be supplanted by his own child, swallowed each of his children by Rhea at birth. Rhea saved the youngest, Zeus, by hiding him and giving Cronus a swaddled stone to swallow instead.

Grown in secret, Zeus forced Cronus to disgorge his swallowed siblings — Hades, Poseidon, Hera, Demeter, and Hestia — and led them in revolt from Mount Olympus against the Titans, who fought from Mount Othrys. The struggle turned when Zeus freed the Cyclopes and the hundred-handed Hecatoncheires from their imprisonment; the Cyclopes forged his thunderbolts, and the Hecatoncheires bombarded the Titans with volleys of stone. Victorious, the Olympians cast the defeated Titans down into Tartarus, the deepest pit of the underworld, and the three brothers drew lots to divide the world: sky to Zeus, sea to Poseidon, and the underworld to Hades. The Titanomachy thus establishes the reign of Zeus and the order of the Olympian world.
