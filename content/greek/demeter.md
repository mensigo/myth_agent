Demeter is the goddess of agriculture, grain, and the harvest, and one of the elder Olympians, daughter of Cronus and Rhea. She governs the fertility of the cultivated earth, and the growth of crops depends on her favor. Her most important myth is the loss of her daughter Persephone, carried off to the underworld by Hades: in her grief Demeter withdrew her gifts, and the earth grew barren until a compromise returned Persephone to her for part of each year — a story the Greeks understood as the origin of the seasons.

Demeter was honored above all in the Eleusinian Mysteries, secret rites at Eleusis that promised initiates a better lot in the afterlife. Her emblems are sheaves of wheat and the torch she carried while searching for her daughter. Through her role in both the harvest and the mysteries, she links the fertility of the fields to human hopes beyond death.
