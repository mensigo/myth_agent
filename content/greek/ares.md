Ares is the god of war, embodying its violence, bloodlust, and raw fury. A son of Zeus and Hera, he represents the brutal, chaotic side of battle, in pointed contrast to Athena, who stands for war conducted with strategy and discipline. The Greeks regarded Ares with a certain distrust and even dislike; in Homer's Iliad Zeus himself calls him the most hateful of the gods, and Ares is wounded and humiliated on the battlefield despite his ferocity.

His most famous myth is his adulterous affair with Aphrodite, wife of Hephaestus, who trapped the lovers in an invisible net and exposed them to the laughter of the other gods. With Aphrodite he fathered Phobos (Fear) and Deimos (Terror), who attend him in war, as well as Harmonia. His attributes are the spear, helmet, and shield, and his animals include the dog and the vulture.
