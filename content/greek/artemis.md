Artemis is the goddess of the hunt, the wilderness, and wild animals, and the twin sister of Apollo, born to Zeus and Leto. A virgin goddess sworn to chastity, she roams the mountains and forests with a band of nymphs, armed with a silver bow. She is called "Mistress of Animals" (Potnia Theron) and protects the young of both beasts and humans, yet she is also a huntress who deals swift death, and in later tradition she is associated with the moon.

Artemis guards her chastity and privacy fiercely: when the hunter Actaeon glimpsed her bathing, she turned him into a stag to be torn apart by his own hounds. She also shares with Apollo the punishment of Niobe, whose boast against their mother Leto cost her all her children to the twins' arrows. Despite her severity, she is invoked as a protector of women in childbirth, having, by one account, helped her mother deliver Apollo soon after her own birth.
