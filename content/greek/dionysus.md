Dionysus is the god of wine, vegetation, ecstatic revelry, and the theatre. He is unusual among the Olympians in being born of a mortal mother: Zeus loved the Theban princess Semele, but Hera tricked her into asking Zeus to reveal himself in his full divine form, and she was consumed by his lightning. Zeus rescued the unborn child and sewed him into his own thigh until he was ready to be born — hence his epithet "twice-born." This mixed origin and his later arrival among the gods give him the character of a god who comes from outside and must win acceptance.

Dionysus travels the world spreading the cultivation of the vine and his own worship, accompanied by ecstatic female followers called maenads and by satyrs. His rites promise liberation from ordinary constraints through wine and frenzy, but they turn deadly for those who reject him: in Euripides' Bacchae, King Pentheus of Thebes scorns the god and is torn apart by the maenads, including his own mother. Dionysus also rescued and married Ariadne after Theseus abandoned her, and from Greek theatrical festivals held in his honor tragedy and comedy were born.
