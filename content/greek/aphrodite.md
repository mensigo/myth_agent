Aphrodite is the goddess of love, beauty, and desire. Her birth is told in two ways: in Hesiod's Theogony she rises from the sea-foam that gathered around the severed genitals of the primordial sky-god Uranus, which is why she is called "foam-born," while Homer instead names her a daughter of Zeus and the Titaness Dione. Either way she embodies the irresistible power of erotic attraction over gods and mortals alike, wielding a magic girdle that makes its wearer desirable.

Though married to the smith-god Hephaestus, she is best known for her affair with Ares, and for her many interventions in mortal love. She awarded Helen to the Trojan prince Paris in exchange for being judged the fairest goddess — the Judgment of Paris — thereby setting off the Trojan War. She loved the beautiful youth Adonis, whose death she mourned deeply, and she was the mother and protector of the Trojan hero Aeneas. Her attributes include the dove, the rose, and the myrtle.
