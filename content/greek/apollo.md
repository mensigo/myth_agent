Apollo is the god of prophecy, music, poetry, healing, and archery, and one of the most widely worshipped of the Olympians. He and his twin sister Artemis are the children of Zeus and the Titaness Leto, born on the island of Delos after Leto wandered in search of a refuge from Hera's jealousy. Apollo is a god of light and order, often identified with the sun in later tradition, and is called Phoebus, "the bright."

His chief sanctuary was Delphi, where his oracle — the priestess known as the Pythia — delivered prophecies believed to come from the god himself; he won the site by slaying the serpent Python. Apollo carries both the lyre, symbol of music and harmony, and the bow, whose arrows can bring sudden death or plague, as at the opening of Homer's Iliad. Though a healer, he is also the father of Asclepius, the god of medicine. His sacred plant is the laurel, tied to the myth of the nymph Daphne, who was transformed into a laurel tree to escape him.
