Hestia is the goddess of the hearth, the home, and the sacred fire that burned at the center of every household and city. The eldest child of Cronus and Rhea — swallowed first and disgorged last — she is a virgin goddess who, according to tradition, refused the courtship of both Poseidon and Apollo and swore to remain unwed, receiving from Zeus the honor of a place at every hearth and a share in every sacrifice.

Because she presides over the hearth fire, Hestia was honored first and last in offerings, and her flame was kept perpetually burning in homes and in the communal hearth of the city (the prytaneum), from which colonists carried fire to new settlements. She figures in few myths and is rarely personified in action, precisely because she represents the stable, unmoving center of domestic and civic life. In some later lists she yields her seat among the twelve Olympians to Dionysus.
