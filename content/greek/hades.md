Hades is the god of the underworld and ruler of the dead. A son of Cronus and Rhea and brother to Zeus and Poseidon, he received the underworld as his domain when the three brothers divided the cosmos after defeating the Titans. He is not a god of death itself but the king of the realm where the dead go, a stern and just ruler who rarely leaves his kingdom and is seldom worshipped directly; the Greeks often avoided speaking his name, calling him instead Plouton ("the wealthy one"), since the riches of the earth come from below.

His best-known myth is the abduction of Persephone, daughter of Demeter, whom he seized to be his queen, leading to the bargain that she spend part of the year with him and part above — the Greek explanation of the seasons. Hades possesses a helm that renders its wearer invisible, and his realm is guarded by the three-headed dog Cerberus, who lets souls in but none out. Few living heroes enter and return: Orpheus nearly won back his wife Eurydice, and Heracles dragged Cerberus up as one of his labors.
