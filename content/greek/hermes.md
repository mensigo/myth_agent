Hermes is the messenger of the gods and one of the most versatile Olympians: god of travelers, trade, herdsmen, eloquence, thieves, and boundaries. He is the son of Zeus and the nymph Maia, and famously precocious — on the day of his birth he climbed from his cradle, invented the lyre from a tortoise shell, and stole Apollo's cattle, later reconciling with Apollo by giving him the lyre. He carries a herald's staff (the caduceus) and wears winged sandals and cap that let him move swiftly between worlds.

As the gods' messenger he delivers Zeus's commands, and as Psychopompos ("guide of souls") he leads the dead down to the underworld, one of the few gods who passes freely between Olympus, earth, and Hades. He is a patron of cleverness and luck, quick-witted and sometimes tricky, and he guides and rescues heroes — giving Perseus aid against Medusa and Odysseus the herb to resist Circe. His epithet Argeiphontes recalls his slaying of the hundred-eyed giant Argus at Zeus's bidding.
