Poseidon is the god of the sea, earthquakes, and horses, and one of the three sons of Cronus and Rhea who divided the world after the fall of the Titans. Where Zeus took the sky, Poseidon received dominion over the sea and all its waters. He is styled the "Earth-shaker," for the Greeks attributed earthquakes to his power, and he is closely linked with horses, which some traditions say he created.

He wields a three-pronged trident, forged by the Cyclopes, with which he can stir storms, split rock, and summon springs. His wife is the sea-nymph Amphitrite. Poseidon appears often as a stern and easily angered god: in Homer's Odyssey he pursues Odysseus with storms for blinding his son, the Cyclops Polyphemus. He also famously contested Athena for patronage of Athens, offering a saltwater spring (or the first horse) while she offered the olive tree — and lost the city's favor.
