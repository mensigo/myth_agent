Persephone is the daughter of Zeus and Demeter and the queen of the underworld. As a young goddess she is called Kore, "the Maiden," associated with the fresh growth of spring. Her defining myth is her abduction: while gathering flowers she was seized by Hades and carried below to be his wife. Her mother Demeter, goddess of the harvest, searched the earth in grief and let the crops fail until Zeus intervened.

A compromise was reached, but because Persephone had eaten a few pomegranate seeds in the underworld — food of the dead that bound her to it — she could not return entirely. She must spend part of each year with Hades below and part with Demeter above; when she is below, Demeter mourns and the earth lies barren (autumn and winter), and when she returns, the world blooms again (spring and summer). Thus Persephone holds a double nature, both the maiden of spring and the dread queen of the dead, and she presides beside Hades over the souls of the departed.
